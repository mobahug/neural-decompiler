## The answer in brief

- **Candidate 1, the protocol: partly new, narrowed.** Preregistration, frozen or hash-committed predictions, single-use
  test sets, kept failures and claim-grading frameworks are each established, and several 2026 studies of model
  internals combine several of them (D1–D3, C1, C13; Buehler, under C). What we did not find is a long sequence of
  preregistered, single-run prospective tests of one mechanistic decomposition, in which each stage was formulated on
  exposed data, frozen as a program under a lock committed before its single confirmation run, and tested once on a
  frozen confirmation set, with errata recorded after the fact, every failure kept and every claim graded on one ladder
  from measurement validity to causal evidence. Not every test passed, and the draft names its exceptions.
- **Candidate 2, the application: partly new, narrowed.** Each ingredient has precedent: programs derived from weights
  and checked beyond their fitting data in small trained models (C3, C6), programs and surrogates for components of
  pretrained models (C2, C11), predictors of an intervention's effects, internal (B6) or on the output (C12),
  conditioned on a measured reference state, held-out predictions of intervention outcomes by fitted alignments (B2,
  B11), and frozen or preset predictions of intervention effects in other settings (D2, C13). What we did not find is
  the combination tested prospectively: for a naturally learned behavior in a pretrained language model, stage programs
  written from the model's weights, each frame's measured reference state and quantities locked from exposed data,
  scored once on new cues from several lexical classes and on new frames, against the internal consequences of an
  intervention on the cue's layer-0 encoding stage by stage, and against the end-to-end contrast change under real cue
  swaps.
- **Candidate 3, the findings: partly new.** A head carrying number as a linear direction to late MLP neurons (A2),
  number detected at the cue token in the same model for verb agreement (A1), and a determiner cue driving a noun's
  number through late MLP units (A3) are known. The specific findings about count-cued noun number in Pythia-70M, each
  at its recorded status, were not found.
- **Candidate 4, the registered boundary: partly new only in its application.** The principle that prediction or
  decoding does not establish the model's causal variables, and that interventional success can mislead unless it acts
  on the aligned variable itself under stated assumptions, is well established (B1, B8, B12, D8–D10), and one
  preregistered study registered decodability and causal efficacy as separate estimands (D1). What remains is best
  folded into candidate 2, and the candidate dropped as a separate one.
- **The gap: partly new.** Cue-to-noun number has been studied by attribution in GPT-2 (A3), and determiner–noun number
  at the neuron level in a masked model, predicting the determiner from the noun (A4); a plural-quantifier feature for
  verb agreement is reported early in the pretraining of a larger Pythia (A12). No work read locates the head- and
  MLP-level mechanism by which an unmodified Pythia-70M reads a numeral or quantifier cue and selects the noun's form,
  or predicts held-out multi-component interventions for that behavior prospectively. The audit's list of close work
  omits A3 and A4. The record itself does not fill the gap's multi-component part
  (research/paper/draft-v0-2026-09-28.md@7520d94:504–506).

None of this is a claim of novelty. Absence in a bounded search is not evidence of absence, and the draft may say only
that we are not aware of such work (research/paper/literature-review/protocol-2026-09-28.md@ee89a7d:99–100).

## How the search ran

- **Searches.** Four read-only agents, one per cluster, searched on 2026-09-28 with web search and page fetching, over
  2019 to September 2026 and the venues the protocol lists. Between them they issued about 135 queries.
- **Seeds.** Every seed was read:

- **Coverage.** About 65 works are entered in full below in 55 entries, some entries grouping papers that share a
  method, and about 55 more were screened at lower relevance.

**Deviations from the protocol,** and what they mean for an entry's weight:
1. **Reading through a fetch tool.** Pages were read with a tool that summarizes a page or answers targeted questions
   about it. An entry marked full text, targeted means that the whole page was fetched and queried section by section,
   not read line by line. A few PDFs were read directly as extracted text (C1, C2, C6, C13, D1, D5). The paraphrases
   therefore rest partly on summaries, which is why the verification pass spot-checks them against the sources.
2. **Blocked or broken pages.** OpenReview pages sat behind a browser check that was not bypassed, so arXiv versions
   were read. Some PDFs could not be parsed, so arXiv HTML or ar5iv versions were read. The Alignment Forum page for
   causal scrubbing was truncated, so a mirror was read (B3).
3. **Numbers** in the entries are as the sources report them, through the fetch tool; none was recomputed.
4. **Grey literature,** an online magazine article and two code repositories, fails the inclusion rule. It is listed
   only under *Screened and excluded* and carries no weight.
5. **The project's own public repository** came up in one search and was excluded.
6. **Relations left implicit.** The protocol asks for each entry's relation to each candidate. Entries in clusters C and
   D do not name candidate 3, several in D do not name candidate 2, and no entry in B–D names the gap. A candidate or
   the gap that an entry does not name is not relevant to it.
7. **Depths the protocol does not name.** *Partial* means that some sections were fetched and queried and others were
   not; for the partial entries here, the search did not record which. *Summary* means that the fetch tool returned one
   summary of the whole page and no section was queried. Entries at these depths are characterized only as far as their
   own lines go.
8. **Two seeds below full text.** A6 and C5 were read at the protocol's floor, abstract, introduction and method, and
   beyond it only in their results (a summary of A6's), although the readiness assessment asked for the close work to be

### Candidate 1, the protocol: partly new, narrowed

**Covered:**
- preregistration and registered reports in machine learning and NLP (D5, D6), and a preregistration template for
  experiments with language-model agents (D4);
- preregistered or frozen-rule studies of model internals that keep their failures, several of them concurrent with the
  end of the sequence:
  - a hash-chained ledger, pre-written indeterminate outcomes and a disclosed post-data fix (D1);
  - a multi-phase design whose paper states that 81 predictions were frozen before their data, with its falsifications
    reported (D2);
  - preregistered kill criteria, prospective predictions on held-out models and per-claim status labels (D3);
  - single-use fresh test sets, preregistered rules and failed routes reported (C1);
  - preset pass-or-fail criteria with failures reported, scored on a toy transformer and followed by a scale-up to nine
    Pythia-70M seeds (C13);
  - endpoints and success rules fixed before their outputs, a failed registered endpoint kept, and stages labeled from
    post hoc to prospective frozen confirmation, in one study of three Gemma models (Buehler, under C);
- hash-recorded predictions, kept as local records, in a behavioral study of language-model networks (Fukushima, under
  D);
- freezing a circuit before its test evaluation (B9), hidden test sets (B11), and pre-specified statistical gates for
  circuit claims, applied retrospectively (B7);
- frameworks for grading interpretability claims by their evidence (D8; Lee and colleagues, under D), and a disclosure
  standard for causal claims (D9).

**Remains:** a long sequence of preregistered, single-run prospective tests of one mechanistic decomposition of one
behavior in one pinned pretrained model, 21 experiments here, with the draft's named exceptions
(research/paper/draft-v0-2026-09-28.md@7520d94:123–141), in which:
- each stage was formulated on exposed data, frozen as a program under a lock committed before its single confirmation
  run, and tested once on a frozen confirmation set; not every test passed, and the composite account was assembled
  afterwards (research/paper/draft-v0-2026-09-28.md@7520d94:39–42);
- errata were recorded after the fact, and every failure was kept across the sequence;
- every claim was graded on one ladder, from measurement validity through localization to causal evidence.

We are not aware of such a sequence.

**Narrowing.** No single practice is the candidate: each is established, and D1 combines several of them in one study of
model internals. The candidate is narrowed to their combination across one sequence that decodes one mechanism stage by
stage. It keeps the draft's record of what the locks held: frame-conditional prediction tables were computed at the
first stage of the single run and held by digest before any target prompt, and none was committed before scoring
(research/paper/draft-v0-2026-09-28.md@7520d94:154–161).

### Candidate 2, the application: partly new, narrowed

**Covered:**
- programs derived from weights and checked beyond their fitting data, in small models trained from scratch (C3, C6);
  weights-to-code extraction from models built for it, or from small recurrent networks, is related but, by its entries,
  distinct from the candidate (C4, C5, C7);
- rules read from the weights of small attention-only language models, without held-out tests (Elhage and colleagues,
  under C), and a weights-based account of one attention head in a pretrained model (McDougall and colleagues, under B);
- programs or symbolic surrogates for components of pretrained models, fitted to activations and tested on held-out text
  and inside the forward pass (C2, C11);
- a program replacing part of a GPT-scale model for a naturally learned behavior, formally verified on a bounded domain,
  in a locally trained and modified model (C1);
- predictors of an intervention's effects conditioned on a measured reference state, in pretrained models:
  - per-prompt replacement models that predict internal downstream effects, validated by perturbations in the real model
    (B6);
  - linear maps from the model's Jacobian that predict the output effect of editing an intermediate encoding, on unseen
    subjects (C12);
  - gradient approximations taken at the clean, unpatched activations that predict patching effects (Kramár and
    colleagues, under C);
  - scores that anticipate which components matter after an ablation (D14);
- held-out predictions of end-to-end outputs under internal interventions by fitted alignments, including prompt
  variants and held-out constructions (B2, B11; Boguraev and colleagues, under B);
- predictions derived from a mechanism of behavior on new kinds of prompts (B4), and qualitative predictions of an
  intervention's downstream effects on later components in another task (Merullo and colleagues, under B);
- frozen predictions of internal quantities after an intervention, in another domain and not shown to be derived from
  weights (D2), and predicted-versus-measured intervention effects under preset criteria, scored only on a toy model
  whose specification was derived from its weights, with the comparison repeated in Pythia-70M but not scored against
  the criteria (C13);
- readable weight-level circuits for naturally learned behaviors, in models trained for weight sparsity (C10);
- predicting downstream values after an intervention on an upstream variable, as one slice of causal abstraction's
  criterion (B1).

**Remains:** for a naturally learned behavior in a pretrained language model, stage programs written from the model's
weights, each frame's measured reference state and quantities locked from exposed data, frozen, then scored once on new
cues from several lexical classes and on new frames:
- against the internal consequences, stage by stage, of an intervention on the cue's layer-0 encoding;
- under real cue swaps, against the end-to-end contrast change.

We are not aware of such a test.

**Narrowing.** The candidate is the combination and its prospective test, not any ingredient. It keeps the draft's own
limits (research/paper/draft-v0-2026-09-28.md@7520d94:460–491), among them:
- the E-patch predictions stop at the head's output, and in new frames they held for most stages, given the frames'
  measured reference states (research/paper/draft-v0-2026-09-28.md@7520d94:52–55);
- under a real swap only `Δc` was scored (research/paper/draft-v0-2026-09-28.md@7520d94:485–486), within the margins and
  failures the draft reports. On the first wiring, later found defective in coordinated frames, it passed by a small
  margin for new cues in exposed frames, with exposed nouns (021:129), pooled over templates, with no positive skill in
  coordinated frames and a negative label in new frames. With block 0 completed, for new determiner-like, quantity and
  adjective cues, three conditions passed relative to the readout's own ceiling, new frames conditional, and one was an
  `ENVELOPE_ONLY_FAILURE`, with no aggregate label (research/paper/draft-v0-2026-09-28.md@7520d94:56–62);
- much of the programs' accuracy may come from exact computation given the measured reference state, for which the
  record has partial, descriptive evidence at block 0 (research/paper/draft-v0-2026-09-28.md@7520d94:471–477). This
  bears directly on how the candidate differs from its closest precedents, B6, C12 and Kramár and colleagues, which also
  condition on a measured state.

B12 bears on the candidate in both directions. The identifiability literature asks for tests on genuinely new inputs,
and new cues and new frames are that kind of test. It also holds that predicting a program's mapped stage variables does
not by itself validate the program as an abstraction of the model.

### Candidate 3, the findings: partly new

**Covered:**
- one attention head carrying a subject's number as a one-dimensional direction to late MLP neurons, for verb agreement
  in Gemma models (A2);
- in Pythia-70M-deduped, number detected at the cue token by embedding and MLP features, moved to the end of the clause
  by boundary-detector features and promoted late, for verb agreement (A1);
- layer-0 involvement in agreement (A5) and a first-layer encoding at the cue (A11);
- a determiner cue driving a noun's number through late MLP value vectors in GPT-2, by attribution (A3);
- number neurons shared across determiners, numerals and quantifiers in a masked model, predicting the determiner from
  the noun (A4);
- a plural-quantifier feature for verb agreement, early in the pretraining of a larger Pythia (A12).

**Remains:** the specific findings about count-cued noun number in Pythia-70M, each at its recorded status:
- `L03.H04`'s selective transport as a linear read-out, with an opposing off-axis part in the encoding of
  singular-selecting determiners;
- a frame-routed population of block-2 MLP neurons carrying an operating-point dependence;
- block-0 attention completing the upstream program;
- a nounness-associated readout error.

We are not aware of prior reports of these.

**Cautions from the literature.** Structurally different circuits can implement interchangeable computations in Pythia
models (Bayat Makou and colleagues, under A), and independently trained Pythia-70M seeds differ by near-random rotations
(C13). The findings, each at its recorded status, concern the one pinned checkpoint, and they do not show that the
components are unique.

### Candidate 4, the registered boundary: partly new only in its application

**Covered:**
- the principle that decoding or prediction does not establish the model's causal variables, and that interventional
  success can mislead unless it acts on the aligned variable itself under stated assumptions:
  - causal abstraction (B1);
  - the subspace-patching illusion (B8);
  - identifiability results (B12);
  - decodability versus use (A10, B9; Ravfogel and Elazar and their colleagues, under B);
  - positions on grading and disclosing claims (D8, D9);
  - an explicitly instrumentalist defense of predicting behavior from components that are not causal (D10);
- its registration: one preregistered study registered decodability and causal efficacy as separate estimands and
  reported them separately (D1);
- explicit caveats in method papers (B3, B6; the claim discipline of C1; C13's warning that its within-model causal and
  predictive passes are tautological).

**Remains:** only the application to a decoded multi-stage program. Its prospective predictions of an intervention's
consequences were scored on frozen sets, while the causal status of the program's own variables was recorded separately
as not established, alongside a preregistered intervention on one decoded direction that did not establish its predicted
effect.

**Narrowing.** As a stand-alone contribution, the candidate narrows to its application; this review recommends dropping
it as a separate candidate (research/paper/literature-review/protocol-2026-09-28.md@ee89a7d:102–103). The boundary stays
the paper's claim discipline, “Prospectively predictive; causal gates unestablished.”
(research/claims/C003-frame-conditioned-decoded-program.md@eeff9ff:5), and should be cited to its precedents (B1, B8,
B12, D1, D8, D10). What remains of it is best presented as part of candidate 2's result.

### A. Grammatical number and agreement mechanisms

The audit's seven close works are A1, A2, A5, A6, A7, A8 and A9. A3 and A4 are not in the audit. A3 is the closest prior
work found on a cue selecting a noun's number; A4 is the nearest found on a number code shared across the draft's cue
classes, in the reverse direction, predicting the determiner from the noun.

**A1. Marks, Rager, Michaud, Belinkov, Bau and Mueller (2025).** *Sparse Feature Circuits: Discovering and Editing
Interpretable Causal Graphs in Language Models.* ICLR 2025. [arXiv 2403.19647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19647). Read:
full text, targeted; the authors' code repository names pythia-70m-deduped.
- *What it does.* Sparse autoencoders are trained on every sublayer of Pythia-70M-deduped, and indirect effects
  estimated with integrated gradients select small feature circuits for four subject–verb agreement templates. In
  Pythia-70M's across-relative-clause circuit, MLP and embedding features detect the subject's number; one pathway
  promotes matching verb forms directly, and another moves the number to the end of the clause through phrase- and
  clause-boundary detector features before verb-form features promote the inflection. Faithfulness and completeness are
  scored on a held-out split.
- *Relation.* Candidate 3 partly overlaps: the same model and the same broad shape (number detected at the cue token,
  moved to the end of the clause, promoted late), but the behavior is verb agreement cued by the subject, the units are
  features rather than heads and neurons, and there are no numeral or quantifier cues. The behavior gap partly overlaps
  for the same reason. Candidates 1, 2 and 4 are distinct: discovery is exploratory, units are selected by attribution,
  and the circuits are presented as causal graphs.

**A2. Ferrando and Costa-jussà (2024).** *On the Similarity of Circuits across Languages: a Case Study on the
Subject-verb Agreement Task.* Findings of EMNLP 2024, pp. 10115–10125. [ACL
Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.591/); [arXiv 2410.06496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06496).
Read: full text, targeted.
- *What it does.* Activation patching and direct logit attribution on subject–verb agreement in Gemma 2B, in English and
  Spanish. One attention head writes the subject's number to the last position as a one-dimensional signal, read mainly
  by one MLP neuron and a few later ones. The English-derived direction flips Spanish verb number when added or
  subtracted, and analogous heads are found in two other Gemma models.
- *Relation.* Candidate 3 partly overlaps: the closest structural analogue found of a single head transporting number as
  a linear read-out to MLP neurons, but in another model, for verb agreement, with the subject noun as the cue; no
  cue-position layer-0 encoding, off-axis part or routing. The gap partly overlaps: a head and MLP number mechanism in
  an unmodified pretrained model, but not for count or quantifier cues and with no held-out predictions. Candidates 1, 2
  and 4 are distinct: the direction's transfer across languages is tested after the fact, and steering is read as causal
  evidence.

**A3. Ferrando, Gállego, Tsiamas and Costa-jussà (2023).** *Explaining How Transformers Use Context to Build
Predictions.* ACL 2023, pp. 5486–5513. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.301/); [arXiv
2305.12535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12535). Read: full text, targeted. Not in the audit.
- *What it does.* A method, ALTI-Logit, attributes the logit difference between a correct and an incorrect next token to
  context tokens across layers, validated on contrastive benchmark pairs (agreement, argument structure and
  negative-polarity licensing) in GPT-2 Small, Large and XL, OPT-125M and two BLOOM models. For determiner–noun
  agreement, the determiner (singular versus plural demonstratives) is the context token that drives the noun's number,
  and in GPT-2 Small a few upper-layer MLP value dimensions consistently raise singular or plural nouns after the
  matching demonstrative. These are identified by their logit contribution, not by intervention.
- *Relation.* Candidate 3 and the gap partly overlap: this is the closest prior study found of a cue selecting a noun's
  number in an autoregressive model, with late MLP units promoting the correct form. It is limited to demonstratives,
  uses GPT-2, and is attribution only, with no head transport, no cue-position encoding and no held-out predictions.

**A4. Niu, Liu, Zhu and Penn (2024).** *What does the Knowledge Neuron Thesis Have to do with Knowledge?* ICLR 2024.

### B. Circuit discovery and causal evaluation of mechanistic hypotheses

Six of the seven leads are in this cluster or the next: B7, B9 and both papers of B10 here, and the two decompilation
leads, C3 and C4, under C. The seventh, D4, is under D. Where a family of papers shares one method, it is entered once.

**B1. Causal abstraction.** Geiger, Lu, Icard and Potts (2021), *Causal Abstractions of Neural Networks*, NeurIPS 2021,
[arXiv 2106.02997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02997), abstract only; and Geiger, Ibeling, Zur, Chaudhary, Chauhan,
Huang, Arora, Wu, Goodman, Potts and Icard (2025), *Causal Abstraction: A Theoretical Foundation for Mechanistic
Interpretability*, JMLR 26, [arXiv 2301.04709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04709), abstract and the definitional sections
of the HTML (the journal PDF could not be parsed).
- *What they do.* A hypothesis is a high-level causal model whose variables are aligned with the network's
  representations, and it is tested by interchange interventions that swap representations between inputs. The 2025
  paper generalizes the theory, defines graded faithfulness through approximate abstraction, of which
  interchange-intervention accuracy, logit difference and KL divergence are instances, and recasts patching, path
  patching, mediation, causal scrubbing, concept erasure, sparse autoencoders, DAS and steering in the language of
  causal abstraction. In the parts read, nothing requires the hypothesis to be fixed before the test data are seen, or
  the test inputs to be new.
- *Relation.* Candidate 2 partly overlaps: predicting downstream values after an intervention on an upstream variable is
  one slice of the framework's criterion; the candidate's programs are written from the weights, conditioned on frames
  and tested prospectively. Candidate 4 partly overlaps: in the framework, a variable earns causal status through
  interventions on that aligned variable, which is consistent with the boundary (a prediction under an upstream
  intervention does not certify the intermediate variables); the framework does not register it. Candidate 1 is
  distinct: it supplies a success criterion, not a sequence of preregistered tests. Candidate 3 is not relevant.

**B2. Distributed alignment search.** Geiger, Wu, Potts, Icard and Goodman (2024), *Finding Alignments Between
Interpretable Causal Variables and Distributed Neural Representations*, CLeaR 2024, PMLR 236, [arXiv
2303.02536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02536), abstract, method and evaluation; and Wu, Geiger, Icard, Potts and Goodman
(2023), *Interpretability at Scale: Identifying Causal Mechanisms in Alpaca*, NeurIPS 2023, [arXiv
2305.08809](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08809), abstract, method and experiments (read under B and C).
- *What they do.* DAS learns a rotation in which a subspace behaves, under interchange interventions, like a variable of
  a hypothesized causal model; the authors note that probes can miss a representation's causal role. Boundless DAS
  scales this to a 7B instruction-tuned model: of four hypothesized causal models of a price-bracket decision, the best
  one's alignment holds on unseen price ranges, with irrelevant context inserted and with a changed output format. The
  authors note that high accuracy does not show it is the only mechanism.
- *Evaluation.* The high-level model is fixed in advance, the rotation is fitted on training pairs, and accuracy is
  measured on held-out pairs and shifted settings. Held out, but fitted, and not preregistered.
- *Relation.* Candidate 2 partly overlaps: held-out predictions of end-to-end outputs under internal interventions,
  including prompt variants that resemble new frames; but the alignment is fitted to intervention data and the
  hypothesis is proposed rather than written from the weights, and the predictions concern swaps of high-level
  variables, not the internal consequences of an intervention stage by stage. Candidate 4 partly overlaps: this is the
  kind of evidence usually read as conferring causal status, which B8 and B12 show can mislead. Candidate 1 is distinct;
  candidate 3 is not relevant.

**B3. Chan, Garriga-Alonso, Goldowsky-Dill, Greenblatt, Nitishinskaya, Radhakrishnan, Shlegeris and Thomas (2022).**
*Causal Scrubbing: a method for rigorously testing interpretability hypotheses.* Alignment Forum (Redwood Research),
December 2022. Read through the [GreaterWrong mirror](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/JvZhhzycHu2Yd57RN), because the
Alignment Forum page returned a truncated excerpt: the main post.
- *What it does.* A hypothesis maps the model's computational graph onto an interpretation graph and is tested by
  resampling ablations that swap activations between inputs the hypothesis treats as equivalent, scored by the fraction
  of loss recovered. The authors state its limits: it cannot separate perfectly correlated features, it can accept
  incomplete explanations, it is not guaranteed to reject false hypotheses, and passing is defeasible evidence.
- *Evaluation.* Hypotheses are refined iteratively on the same data; no held-out or preregistered step is described.
- *Relation.* Candidate 4 partly overlaps: passing is said not to establish the true mechanism, but this is not
  registered. Candidate 1 partly overlaps only in framing interpretability as hypothesis testing. Candidate 2 is
  distinct: it checks that behavior is preserved under resampling, not predictions of an intervention's consequences in
  new frames. Candidate 3 is not relevant.

**B4. Wang, Variengien, Conmy, Shlegeris and Steinhardt (2023).** *Interpretability in the Wild: a Circuit for Indirect
Object Identification in GPT-2 small.* ICLR 2023. [arXiv 2211.00593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00593). Read: abstract,
method, evaluation and the adversarial-example section (under B and D). With it, Goldowsky-Dill, MacLeod, Sato and Arora
(2023), *Localizing Model Behavior with Path Patching*, [arXiv 2304.05969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05969), abstract
only, which formalizes path patching as a test of localization hypotheses.
- *What it does.* Path patching isolates a circuit of 26 heads in seven classes (the arXiv version; the ICLR 2023
  abstract gives 28), scored for faithfulness (about 87% of the logit difference), completeness and minimality; backup
  heads take over when the main ones are ablated. From the circuit, the authors predict that duplicating the indirect
  object's name will break the behavior, and on such prompts the wrong name is chosen far more often than at baseline;
  they concede such prompts might have been found without the circuit.
- *Evaluation.* The criteria are measured on the discovery distribution; the adversarial prediction is on a new input
  class but was not preregistered.
- *Relation.* Candidate 2 partly overlaps: a mechanism predicts end-to-end behavior on a new kind of prompt, but the
  prediction is behavioral and not written from the weights, with no stage-wise internal predictions. Candidate 4 partly
  overlaps: a faithful circuit can be incomplete and compensated by backup heads, which parallels C002's gap between
  exact replacement and isolation. Candidate 1 is distinct; candidate 3 is not relevant.

**B5. Automated circuit discovery.** Conmy, Mavor-Parker, Lynch, Heimersheim and Garriga-Alonso (2023), *Towards
Automated Circuit Discovery for Mechanistic Interpretability*, NeurIPS 2023, [arXiv
2304.14997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14997), abstract, method and evaluation; Syed, Rager and Conmy (2024),
*Attribution Patching Outperforms Automated Circuit Discovery*, BlackboxNLP 2024, [arXiv
2310.10348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10348), abstract only; Hanna, Pezzelle and Belinkov (2024), *Have Faith in
Faithfulness: Going Beyond Circuit Overlap When Finding Model Mechanisms*, COLM 2024, [arXiv
2403.17806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17806), abstract only.
- *What they do.* ACDC prunes edges whose removal keeps a divergence below a threshold and is scored by how well it
  recovers published circuits; it misses negative components and depends on the corruption distribution and the metric.
  Edge attribution patching approximates this linearly at far lower cost; an integrated-gradients variant finds more
  faithful circuits, and its authors argue that circuits should be judged by faithfulness rather than by overlap with
  earlier circuits.
- *Evaluation.* Discovery and evaluation within one task distribution, against reference circuits that were themselves
  found post hoc; no transfer tests in the text read.
- *Relation.* Distinct from candidates 1, 2 and 3; candidate 4 partly overlaps only in the point that overlap with a
  reference circuit is not faithfulness.

**B6. Circuit tracing.** Ameisen, Lindsey, Pearce, Gurnee, Turner, Chen, Citro and colleagues (2025), *Circuit Tracing:
Revealing Computational Graphs in Language Models*, Transformer Circuits Thread, March 2025,
[transformer-circuits.pub](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html), full page (under B)
and abstract, introduction and method (under C); and Lindsey, Gurnee, Ameisen, Chen and colleagues (2025), *On the
Biology of a Large Language Model*, Transformer Circuits Thread, March 2025,
[transformer-circuits.pub](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html), partial.
- *What they do.* A cross-layer transcoder replaces the MLPs; for each prompt, a local replacement model freezes that
  prompt's attention patterns and normalization and adds error terms, so that effects between features become linear and
  can be traced as an attribution graph. Graphs are validated by perturbing features in the real model: graph influence
  and measured ablation effects correlate with Spearman 0.72, and perturbation effects in the replacement and the real
  model agree at about 0.8 cosine one layer after the intervention, with discrepancies compounding over layers. The
  companion paper tests graph-derived hypotheses by interventions in a production model, reports satisfying insight for
  about a quarter of the prompts tried, and cautions that conclusions about the replacement model do not carry over to
  the underlying model with certainty.
- *Evaluation.* Hypotheses are read off graphs built on the same prompts and then tested by interventions, some with
  features taken from other prompts; not preregistered, and the cases shown are selected successes.
- *Relation.* Candidate 2 partly overlaps and is the closest analogue in the literature read: predictions of the
  internal downstream consequences of an intervention in a pretrained model, conditioned on each prompt's measured
  forward state and checked by perturbations in the real model, with attention patterns held at their unperturbed
  values. The graphs are prompt-specific and built on a trained replacement basis rather than written from the model's
  own weights, there are no systematic prospective tests on new cues or frames, and agreement decays with depth.
  Candidate 4 partly overlaps through its explicit caveat, which is not registered. Candidate 1 is distinct, with
  selection of successes explicit; candidate 3 is not relevant.

**B7. Shi, Beltran-Velez, Nazaret, Zheng, Garriga-Alonso, Jesson, Makar and Blei (2024).** *Hypothesis Testing the
Circuit Hypothesis in LLMs.* NeurIPS 2024. [arXiv 2410.13032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13032). Read: full text,
targeted (under B and D). A lead.
- *What it does.* Formalizes three idealized circuit properties, each with a statistical test (mechanism preservation,
  localization and minimality), and adds graded sufficiency and partial-necessity tests against random circuits. The two
  compiled circuits pass all three. Of the four published circuits, the induction circuit passes two tests and the
  docstring circuit one, both found in small toy transformers; the indirect-object and greater-than circuits in GPT-2
  small pass none. On the graded tests all four are more faithful than random circuits, even far larger ones, and
  removing them hurts more than removing random circuits drawn from their complement, but not always more than removing
  a random circuit of the same size drawn from the whole model. The authors warn that, like other hypothesis tests,
  theirs are open to misuse such as p-hacking, and that their inferences assume one fixed experimental setup and can
  differ across setups.
- *Evaluation.* Retrospective, on the published circuits' own data.
- *Relation.* Candidate 1 partly overlaps: pre-specified statistical pass-or-fail gates for mechanistic claims, but
  applied retrospectively, on the same data, with no sequence or ladder. Candidate 4 partly overlaps: it separates
  faithfulness from localization and minimality, not predictive success from causal status. Candidate 2 is distinct.
  Candidate 3 is distinct in findings; methodologically it parallels C002's necessity and sufficiency gates.

**B8. Makelov, Lange and Nanda (2024).** *Is This the Subspace You Are Looking For? An Interpretability Illusion for
Subspace Activation Patching.* ICLR 2024. [arXiv 2311.17030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17030). Read: full text,
targeted. With it, the reply by Wu, Geiger, Huang, Arora, Icard, Potts and Goodman (2024), *A Reply to Makelov et al.
(2023)'s ‘Interpretability Illusion’ Arguments*, [arXiv 2401.12631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12631), full text,
targeted.
- *What they do.* Patching a one-dimensional subspace can mimic a feature change by combining a direction that varies
  across inputs but is causally disconnected with one that is connected but normally dormant. The illusion is shown in a
  toy model and in two tasks in GPT-2 models; in one of them, evaluating on held-out names and templates did not remove
  it. The authors recommend nullspace and rowspace checks, agreement with directions derived from a circuit, and
  patching at residual-stream bottlenecks. The reply argues that the definition also flags valid explanations, and that
  interchange-intervention accuracy, though limited, tracks counterfactual behavior where the critique's
  logit-difference metric does not.
- *Relation.* Candidate 4 overlaps in argument: it is the closest prior argument found that held-out interventional
  success does not by itself establish the model's variable, though contested and not registered. Candidate 2 is
  distinct, but the work bears on its intervention design: its analysis concerns subspace patches, while the E-patch
  replaces the whole layer-0 MLP output at the cue position; 025's rotation of the cue embedding along a decoded
  direction is a subspace intervention, to which its diagnostics apply. Candidate 1 is distinct; candidate 3 is not
  relevant.

**B9. Darade and Thorat (2026).** *Represented Is Not Computed: A Causal Test of Candidate Algorithmic Intermediates in
a Transformer.* arXiv preprint (v1, May 2026). [arXiv 2605.22488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22488). Read: full text,
targeted. A lead.
- *What it does.* Ten-layer transformers trained from scratch, three seeds, on a digit-extraction task: linear probes
  decode the closed-form intermediates almost perfectly, suggesting staged arithmetic, but key and value patching shows
  that the route into the output is selective for one input only, and a sparse route circuit has separate pathways that
  combine late. The conclusion is that high decodability need not mark a causal intermediate.
- *Evaluation.* Probes and interventions on held-out input combinations; circuits found on each seed's validation split
  and frozen before the test evaluation; not preregistered.
- *Relation.* Candidate 4 partly overlaps: it dissociates decodability from causal transmission; the boundary concerns
  the further case in which an intervention's consequences are prospectively predicted and the variables' causal status
  is still not established. Candidate 1 partly overlaps only in discovering, freezing and then testing. Candidate 2 is

**C1. Somani (2026).** *Towards Verifiable Transformers: Solver-Checkable Circuit Explanations.* arXiv preprint (v2,

**C2. Hayes, Li and Andreas (2026).** *Explaining Attention with Program Synthesis.* arXiv preprint (v2, June 2026).

**D1. Xun (2026).** *Lagged Coupling: Internal Representations Become Readable Before They Become Causal.* arXiv
preprint (v1, September 2026); its arXiv record describes a pre-registered study with a replication, and names no venue.

**D2. Zhu (2026).** *Reading the Gate, Not the Interference: Output-Side Interference Measurement Does Not Track Merge
Collapse.* arXiv preprint (v3, September 2026). [arXiv 2608.11797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11797). Read: full text,
targeted.
- *What it does.* Asks whether activation interference between merged task vectors carries the collapse of merged
  models, in a 1.5B model with replications in two others. The work runs in phases, each with thresholds frozen in a
  versioned freeze log before its data: numerical validity gates, a decomposition with identity checks, erasure with
  frozen controls, regrowth windows, gating and behavioral validation. The paper states that 81 predictions, across the
  study and its follow-up preregistrations, were frozen before their data, each with its own hash file released with the
  code; the paper gives no repository link, and the files were not inspected. It reports its falsifications, including
  two of three headline expectations; of the original 14 predictions, 7 held, 4 failed and 3 split. Erasure shows the
  interference term is causally structured but plays no causal part in collapse.
- *Relation.* Candidate 1 partly overlaps: a multi-phase sequence of frozen predictions about model internals with
  falsifications kept, but in another domain, not a stage-by-stage decoding of one mechanism, and with no evidence
  ladder or independent phase-gate review found. Candidate 4 partly overlaps: correlational tracking and the causal role
  shown by removal are kept apart, without a registered labeling rule. Candidate 2 partly overlaps: frozen predictions
  of internal quantities after an intervention, not shown to be derived from weights.

**D3. Dong (2026).** *The Kinetics of Training: A Driven-Nucleation Rate Law for Emergence, Plasticity Loss, and Circuit
Control in Language Models.* arXiv preprint (July 2026). [arXiv 2607.27281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27281). Read:
abstract and full text, one targeted pass.
- *What it does.* Proposes that a capability forms only when its circuit components align jointly. Protocols with pass
  and kill criteria were frozen before the formal runs; a constant fitted on five Pythia models and then frozen
  predicted the onset timing of induction for six held-out models within a preregistered band. Contaminated cells are
  withdrawn in the open, a complete failure log is kept, including one failed frozen prediction, and every claim is
  labeled as cited, proved or measured, with one signal explicitly classed as correlational rather than mechanistic.
- *Relation.* Candidate 1 partly overlaps: preregistered prospective predictions on held-out models, kill criteria, kept
  negatives and claim-status labels; but a law of training dynamics rather than a decoding of one mechanism, and a
  labeling scheme that is not a ladder from measurement validity to causal evidence. Candidate 4 partly overlaps in
  reporting, not as a registered rule for a mechanism program. Candidate 2 is distinct.

**D4. Vaccaro (2026).** *Position: Preregister Experiments with AI Agents.* ICML 2026 position paper (spotlight), PMLR
306, pp. 172351–172364; the arXiv version is titled *Preregistration for Experiments with AI Agents*. [arXiv
2606.11217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11217). Read: full text, targeted. A lead.
- *What it does.* Argues that behavioral experiments with language models and agents combine near-zero cost per run with
  a very large space of analysis choices, so that undisclosed forking paths are easy to take and hard to detect, and

- *Relation.* Candidate 1 partly overlaps on principles, for behavioral experiments only, as a template rather than an
  executed sequence, with no mechanism, ladder or hash locks. Candidates 2 and 4 are not relevant.

**D5. Bertinetto, Henriques, Albanie, Paganini and Varol (2021).** Preface to the NeurIPS 2020 Workshop on

**D7. Bai, Baumgartner, Sun, Holtzman and Tan (2026).** *The Story is Not the Science: Execution-Grounded Evaluation of

2602.18458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18458). Read: abstract and full text, targeted.
- *What it does.* Argues that reviewing only the paper misses failures that appear when code and data are run, and

**D8. Joshi, Mueller, Klindt, Brendel, Reizinger and Sridhar (2026).** *Position: Causality Is Key for Interpretability
Claims to Generalise.* ICML 2026, position paper track, PMLR 306, pp. 170741–170779; the arXiv record (v2, March 2026)
lists the title without the prefix, which the v2 PDF carries. [arXiv 2602.16698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16698).
Read: full text, targeted (under B and D).
- *What it does.* Places interpretability evidence on the three levels of Pearl's causal hierarchy: probing,
  decodability and reconstruction are associational; ablation, patching and steering are interventional and support
  average-effect claims on the prompts tested; counterfactual claims are mostly unverifiable without strong assumptions.
  It offers a diagnostic and a checklist for matching claims to evidence, and a pilot annotation finds that about half
  of the sampled claims can be read more strongly than their evidence supports.
- *Relation.* Candidate 1 partly overlaps: an explicit ladder for grading interpretability claims, whose rungs are
  Pearl's rather than measurement validity, localization and causal evidence, and which is not tied to preregistered
  tests. Candidate 4 partly overlaps: predictive or decoding success does not license causal claims, as a diagnostic
  rather than a separation registered in a test program.

**D9. Lin and Liu (2026).** *Position: Mechanistic Interpretability Must Disclose Identification Assumptions for Causal
Claims.* arXiv preprint (May 2026); its record lists a submission to the NeurIPS 2026 position track, acceptance not
confirmed. [arXiv 2605.08012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08012). Read: full text, targeted.
- *What it does.* A single-coder, full-text audit of 10 papers across circuit discovery, sparse autoencoders, causal
  abstraction and probing finds none with a dedicated section on identification assumptions, and 8 of 10 substituting
  validation metrics for identification fully or partly. A two-coder audit of 30 papers, coded from abstracts and
  metadata only, points the same way. It proposes a disclosure standard: whether a claim is causal, the identification
  strategy, the numbered assumptions, falsification or sensitivity tests, and how the conclusions change if the
  assumptions fail.
- *Relation.* Candidates 1 and 4 partly overlap, as a reporting norm rather than preregistered tests or a separation
  registered in an empirical program.

**D10. Li, Kaufmann, Qin, Wattenberg, Alvarez-Melis and Saphra (2025).** *Can Interpretation Predict Behavior on Unseen
Data?* arXiv preprint (v4, September 2026); no archival venue was found, and the last author's CV lists presentations at
NeurIPS workshops in 2024 and 2025. [arXiv 2507.06445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06445). Read: full text, targeted
(under B and D).
- *What it does.* Hundreds of small transformers are trained so that the training data fit two generalization rules
  equally well. Attention patterns measured in distribution predict, well above chance, which rule a model follows out
  of distribution. Ablations show that some types of predictive head suppress, rather than support, the rule they
  predict, and the authors take an explicitly instrumentalist stance: predicting behavior on unseen data is valuable
  even when the interpreted component is not causal.
- *Relation.* Candidate 4 partly overlaps and is the clearest conceptual statement found: predictive validity and causal
  implementation are separate. It is not registered, has no claim labels, and predicts behavioral rules across
  populations of toy models. Candidate 2 partly overlaps: predictions from internals for unseen inputs, derived from
  activations and targeting behavior rather than an intervention's internal consequences. Candidate 1 partly overlaps,
  weakly.

**D12. Friedman, Lampinen, Dixon, Chen and Ghandeharioun (2024).** *Interpretability Illusions in the Generalization of
Simplified Models.* ICML 2024. [arXiv 2312.03656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03656). Read: abstract and full text,

**D13. Rushing and Nanda (2024).** *Explorations of Self-Repair in Language Models.* ICML 2024. [arXiv
2402.15390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15390). Read: full text, targeted. In the design's neighborhood.
- *What it does.* Measures self-repair as the gap between a head's direct effect and the logit change under resample

**D14. Gong, Lu, Wang, Zhang, Wang, Zeng, Xiao, Yuen and Lim (2026).** *Conditional Co-Ablation: Recovering Self-Repair

2607.01940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01940). Read: abstract and full text, one targeted pass. In the design's

- *Relation.* Candidate 2 partly overlaps and is the closest work found in this cluster: it anticipates which components
  become important after an intervention and checks this on held-out prompts, from activations and ablations rather than
  programs written from the weights. Candidate 4 partly overlaps, indirectly. Candidate 1 is distinct.
